# A day with Lena: OpenWork right next to the tools you already use

**Video:** `lena-day-v3.mp4` (narrated)

Lena Hoffmann is an account manager at Acme Robotics. Today she has a customer question to answer and an account review to prepare, while the usual small requests keep coming in. She asks in OpenWork, and the results land where she already works: Outlook, OneDrive, Jira and Confluence.

Acme Robotics, its customers (Northwind Logistics, Blue Harbor Foods) and its people are fictional sample data.

---

## 1. Her morning brief runs by itself

| | |
|---|---|
| **09:00** | Her morning brief Automation has just run. A news summary for France, the US, Germany and Japan is waiting in her Outlook drafts. |
| **She asks** | *"Anything in this morning's brief that matters for Blue Harbor or Northwind?"* |
| **She gets** | A short answer that ties each story to her accounts, and says plainly when nothing changes her priorities. |

**How it was set up.** She asked once, in one sentence:

> *"Every weekday at 9am, put a short news brief for France, the US, Germany and Japan in a draft email for me."*

OpenWork drafted an **Automation** for her to review: weekdays at 09:00, what to read, where the brief goes. She clicked **Create Automation**, and it shows as *Active* in Automations. **Run now** runs it immediately; otherwise it runs on schedule.

## 2. Organise the day

> *"Set up To do, In progress and Done folders in Outlook and sort my inbox into them."*

The three folders appear in Outlook, and her email is sorted into them.

## 3. A customer question becomes something she can reuse

**Blue Harbor asks which robot fits.**

> *"Blue Harbor wants to know how Atlas 2 and Atlas Lift compare for their pallet moves. Can you make me a one-slide summary and draft a reply to Mateo?"*

- OpenWork reads both product datasheets and the email thread.
- It builds a one-slide PowerPoint in OneDrive: a recommendation, a side-by-side table, and the open safety points.
- It leaves an unsent reply to Mateo in the same thread.

**Save it as a skill.**

> *"That's exactly what I needed. Save it as a skill so I can do this for other customers."*

The skill keeps what made it work: which pages to read, the slide template, and what the reply should contain.

**Use it for the next customer.** Northwind asks almost the same thing. Lena picks her skill from the **/** menu (it shows as a chip on her message) and says only what's different:

> */customer-product-comparison* · *"Northwind is asking the same thing for moving totes from the dock to the line."*

**Share it.**

> *"Nice. Share that skill with Nora."*

## 4. Prepare the account review

> *"Make me a short deck for tomorrow's review of Northwind and Blue Harbor."*

A short PowerPoint lands in OneDrive. The video shows the slides themselves.

## 5. Small things keep coming in

| Request | What happens |
|---|---|
| *"Can you ask Nora to update the Project XYZ docs? Make it a Jira task."* | OpenWork creates the task in the Documentation project and assigns it to Nora. |
| *"I'm locked out of Windows. Can you open an IT ticket for me?"* | The right IT request is filed with **Windows login** as the application. OpenWork doesn't touch her password. |
| *"Can I bring my dog to the office?"* | The handbook doesn't say, so OpenWork tells her that and suggests People Ops. No made-up policy. |
| *"Book 30 minutes with Nora and Priya on Monday at 10 to go over the Atlas comparison."* → *"Actually, can you move it to Tuesday at 11?"* | One meeting, moved to the new time, both colleagues invited. |

## 6. Close the loop

> *"I've answered Blue Harbor and Northwind — move those threads to Done."*

## 7. Going further: a live app

> *"Can you build me a small app that shows my two pilots — units, value and status — from the forecast in OneDrive?"*

A **Pilot forecast** app opens next to the chat. **Refresh forecast** pulls the latest numbers from the spreadsheet.

## 8. Stay up to date with your team

The next morning Nora opens **Activity**. She sees the skill Lena shared (with **Try it**) and a connector that was just made available to her.

### What to point out when presenting

- **The prompts are what a person actually types.** One or two sentences each. Lena's context and preferences live in her workspace: her customers, PowerPoint and Word rather than Markdown, never send.
- **OpenWork is where she asks; her tools are where the work lands.** Each step ends with a quick look at Outlook, OneDrive, Jira or the slides.
- **Lena stays in control.** Emails stay drafts. The IT ticket is a request. She reviews the Automation before creating it. When something isn't documented, she's told.
- **Reuse compounds.** A skill turns one good result into a one-line request, and her team sees it in Activity.
